\begin{abstract}
\setlength{\parindent}{1.5em}
\indent 

Dancing with the Stars (DWTS) combines professional judge scores with audience votes to determine weekly eliminations, yet the actual fan vote distribution remains a closely guarded secret. This paper develops a comprehensive mathematical framework to estimate fan votes and analyze the voting system's properties across 34 seasons of data.

\textbf{For Problem 1}, we formulate fan vote estimation as a constrained optimization problem, where elimination outcomes serve as constraints. Using the maximum entropy principle, we minimize deviation from uniform distribution while satisfying elimination constraints. Our model achieves an overall consistency rate of 93.9\%, with the percentage-based method (Seasons 3-27) yielding 98.5\% accuracy compared to 80.3\% for the rank-based method. The average certainty score is 0.273 $\pm$ 0.142, reflecting inherent solution non-uniqueness.

\textbf{For Problem 2}, we compare rank-based and percentage-based voting methods across all seasons. Results show 89.8\% agreement between methods, with percentage-based method slightly favoring judges (bias = 0.094 vs 0.039). Analysis of controversial contestants (Jerry Rice, Bristol Palin, Bobby Bones) reveals that their survival was due to genuinely high fan support rather than voting system artifacts. The judge tiebreaker rule would alter approximately 40\% of elimination outcomes.

\textbf{For Problem 3}, we employ Ridge regression and Random Forest to analyze factor impacts. Age shows the strongest negative correlation with performance ($r = -0.424$ for judge scores, $r = -0.338$ for fan votes). Professional dancer assignment significantly affects judge scores ($F = 3.62$, $p < 0.0001$) but less so for fan votes ($F = 1.60$, $p = 0.02$).

\textbf{For Problem 4}, we propose a Dynamic Weighted Voting System (DWVS) with formula $S_{final} = \alpha \cdot P_{judge} + (1-\alpha) \cdot P_{fan}$. Parameter optimization reveals that $\alpha \in [0.05, 0.45]$ achieves 76.1\% consistency, a 9-12 percentage point improvement over existing methods. From a fairness perspective, we recommend $\alpha = 0.5$ for balanced consideration of professional judgment and audience preference.

\begin{keywords}
Constrained Optimization; Inverse Problem; Fan Vote Estimation; Voting System Analysis; Dynamic Weighted Voting
\end{keywords}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Problem Background}

Dancing with the Stars (DWTS) is the American adaptation of the international television franchise ``Strictly Come Dancing,'' which has captivated audiences across more than 60 countries. The U.S. version, having completed 34 seasons, pairs celebrity contestants with professional ballroom dancers for weekly performances judged by a panel of experts and voted on by viewers.

The show's elimination mechanism combines judge scores with audience votes, creating a complex interplay between professional evaluation and popular appeal. While judge scores are publicly disclosed (ranging from 1-10 per judge), the actual distribution of fan votes remains confidential, presenting an intriguing mathematical challenge: can we reverse-engineer the voting patterns that led to observed elimination outcomes?

Throughout its history, DWTS has employed two primary voting combination methods. Seasons 1-2 and 28-34 used a rank-based approach where judge and fan rankings are summed, with the highest total rank eliminated. Seasons 3-27 adopted a percentage-based method where judge score percentages and fan vote percentages are combined, with the lowest total percentage eliminated. This rule change followed the controversial Season 2 outcome involving Jerry Rice, who reached the finals despite consistently low judge scores.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
% 问题重述
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Problem Restatement}

We are tasked with developing analytical models to address the following research questions:

\textbf{Problem 1:} Develop a mathematical model to estimate weekly fan votes for each contestant. Verify whether estimated votes correctly predict elimination outcomes and quantify the certainty of these estimates.

\textbf{Problem 2:} Compare and contrast the rank-based and percentage-based voting combination methods across all seasons. Analyze whether one method favors fan votes more than the other, examine controversial cases (Jerry Rice, Billy Ray Cyrus, Bristol Palin, Bobby Bones), and evaluate the impact of the judge tiebreaker rule introduced in Season 28.

\textbf{Problem 3:} Analyze the impact of professional dancers and celebrity characteristics (age, industry) on competition performance. Determine whether these factors affect judge scores and fan votes differently.

\textbf{Problem 4:} Design a new voting system that is more ``fair'' or better in some other way. Provide justification for why the proposed system should be adopted.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
% 我们的方法
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Our Approach}

We develop a comprehensive analytical framework that addresses all four tasks through a combination of optimization theory, statistical analysis, and machine learning techniques.

For Problem 1, we formulate fan vote estimation as an inverse problem solved through constrained optimization, applying the maximum entropy principle to find the most likely vote distribution consistent with observed eliminations.

For Problem 2, we implement both voting methods programmatically and apply them to all 264 elimination weeks, quantifying agreement rates, bias metrics, and the impact of the judge tiebreaker rule.

For Problem 3, we employ correlation analysis, Ridge regression, Random Forest feature importance, and ANOVA to systematically evaluate factor impacts on both judge scores and fan votes.

For Problem 4, we propose a Dynamic Weighted Voting System (DWVS) with tunable parameters, conducting extensive parameter sensitivity analysis to identify optimal configurations.

\subsection{Model Assumptions}

\textbf{Assumption 1:} The elimination process strictly follows the announced voting combination rules (rank-based or percentage-based) without external interference.

\textbf{Justification:} While production decisions may occasionally influence outcomes, we assume the stated rules are applied consistently, as this is the basis for meaningful analysis.

\textbf{Assumption 2:} Fan vote proportions are bounded between 0 and 1, with the sum across all contestants in a given week equal to 1.

\textbf{Justification:} This reflects the fundamental properties of vote share distributions and enables meaningful comparison across weeks with different total vote counts.

\textbf{Assumption 3:} In weeks without eliminations, fan vote distributions are positively correlated with judge scores.

\textbf{Justification:} While fan preferences may diverge from judges, in the absence of elimination constraints, we apply this reasonable prior based on general voting behavior patterns.

\textbf{Assumption 4:} Celebrity characteristics (age, industry) remain constant throughout a season and affect performance consistently across weeks.

\textbf{Justification:} While performance may vary weekly, the baseline influence of demographic factors is assumed stable for regression analysis.

\subsection{Data Overview and Preprocessing}

The provided dataset contains 421 celebrity contestants across 34 seasons, with weekly judge scores from 3-4 judges (1-10 scale per judge). Key preprocessing steps include:

\textbf{Missing Value Handling:} ``N/A'' values appear for non-existent judge slots (when only 3 judges present) and weeks beyond a season's duration. A score of 0 indicates elimination in a previous week. We filter these cases appropriately for each analysis.

\textbf{Score Normalization:} Some weeks contain decimal scores (e.g., 8.5) due to multiple dances being averaged, or bonus points from dance-offs. We use these values directly without further normalization.

\textbf{Feature Engineering:} We derive additional features including:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{voting\_method}: ``rank'' for Seasons 1-2, 28-34; ``percentage'' for Seasons 3-27
\item \texttt{elimination\_week}: The week in which a contestant was eliminated
\item \texttt{is\_finalist}: Boolean indicating whether contestant reached the finale
\item \texttt{active\_weeks}: Number of weeks a contestant participated
\item \texttt{overall\_avg\_score}: Average judge score across all active weeks
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Data Transformation:} We convert the wide-format data (one row per contestant) to long format (one row per contestant-week) for weekly analysis, resulting in 2,777 active contestant-week records.

\section{Problem 1: Fan Vote Estimation Model}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

The fan vote estimation problem is fundamentally an \textbf{inverse problem}: given observed outcomes (eliminations) and partial information (judge scores), we seek to reconstruct the hidden variable (fan vote distribution). The key constraint is that the estimated votes must produce elimination outcomes consistent with actual results.

\subsection{Methodology}

\subsubsection{Rank-Based Method (Seasons 1-2, 28-34)}

Under the rank-based combination rule, contestant $i$'s total rank is:
\begin{equation}
R_{total,i} = R_{judge,i} + R_{fan,i}
\label{eq:rank_method}
\end{equation}
where $R_{judge,i}$ is the rank based on total judge score (1 = highest score) and $R_{fan,i}$ is the rank based on fan votes (1 = most votes). The contestant with the highest $R_{total}$ is eliminated.

For a given week with eliminated contestant $e$, we seek fan vote proportions $\{V_i\}$ such that $R_{total,e} \geq R_{total,j}$ for all $j \neq e$. We use a heuristic approach that assigns fan ranks to satisfy this constraint while maintaining correlation with judge rankings.

\subsubsection{Percentage-Based Method (Seasons 3-27)}

Under the percentage-based rule, contestant $i$'s total percentage is:
\begin{equation}
P_{total,i} = P_{judge,i} + P_{fan,i} = \frac{J_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} J_j} + V_i
\label{eq:pct_method}
\end{equation}
where $\sum_{i=1}^{n} V_i = 1$. The contestant with the lowest $P_{total}$ is eliminated.

We formulate this as a constrained optimization problem using the maximum entropy principle:

\textbf{Objective Function:} Minimize deviation from uniform distribution
\begin{equation}
\min_{V} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( V_i - \frac{1}{n} \right)^2
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Subject to:}
\begin{align}
\sum_{i=1}^{n} V_i &= 1 \quad \text{(normalization)} \\
V_i &\geq 0 \quad \text{(non-negativity)} \\
P_{judge,e} + V_e &\leq P_{judge,j} + V_j - \epsilon \quad \forall j \neq e \quad \text{(elimination constraint)}
\end{align}

where $\epsilon = 0.001$ is a small margin ensuring strict inequality. We solve this using Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP).

To validate our model, we verify whether the estimated fan votes correctly identify the eliminated contestant in each week.

As shown in Table \ref{tab:consistency}, the model achieves high overall consistency, with the percentage-based method performing substantially better than the rank-based method. \subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}

We quantify the certainty of our estimates using a composite metric:
\begin{equation}
C = 0.3 \times (1 - H_{norm}) + 0.3 \times 5 \times G + 0.4 \times \frac{\rho + 1}{2}
\label{eq:certainty}
\end{equation}

where $H_{norm}$ is the normalized entropy of the vote distribution (measuring concentration), $G$ is the gap between the lowest and second-lowest vote proportions, and $\rho$ is the Spearman correlation between vote and score rankings.

\section{Problem 2: Voting Method Comparison}

\subsection{Methodology}

We implement both voting combination methods and apply them to all 264 elimination weeks across 34 seasons, using the fan vote estimates from Problem 1. For each week, we determine:
\begin{itemize}
\item Which contestant would be eliminated under the rank-based method
\item Which contestant would be eliminated under the percentage-based method
\item Whether the two methods agree
\end{itemize}

We also analyze the ``bias'' of each method using:
\begin{equation}
Bias = \frac{R_{judge,elim} - R_{fan,elim}}{n}
\label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
Positive bias indicates the eliminated contestant had worse judge rank than fan rank (method favors judges); negative bias indicates the opposite.

\subsection{Recommendations}

Based on our analysis, we recommend the \textbf{percentage-based method} for future seasons for the following reasons:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Higher consistency}: The percentage method achieves 67.0\% consistency with actual outcomes versus 64.4\% for the rank method.
\item \textbf{Transparency}: Percentages are more intuitive for viewers than rank sums.
\item \textbf{Continuity}: Percentages provide smoother differentiation between contestants with similar rankings.
\end{enumerate}

Regarding the judge tiebreaker rule, we suggest \textbf{cautious implementation}. While it increases judge influence, its effectiveness depends on judges' actual decision criteria, which may prioritize entertainment value over pure merit.

\section{Problem 3: Factor Impact Analysis}

\subsection{Methodology}

We analyze the impact of celebrity characteristics and professional dancer assignments on competition performance using multiple statistical approaches:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pearson Correlation}: For continuous variables (age vs. performance)
\item \textbf{Ridge Regression}: To estimate factor coefficients with regularization
\item \textbf{Random Forest}: For non-linear feature importance estimation
\item \textbf{ANOVA}: To test categorical factor effects (industry, professional dancer)
\end{enumerate}

Our response variables are \texttt{overall\_avg\_score} (average judge score across active weeks) and \texttt{avg\_vote\_prop} (average estimated fan vote proportion).

\section{Problem 4: New Voting System Design}

\subsection{Design Principles}

Based on our analysis of existing systems, we identify four key principles for a new voting system:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fairness}: Balance professional judgment with audience preference
\item \textbf{Transparency}: Rules should be simple and easily understood
\item \textbf{Incentive Alignment}: Reward genuine performance quality
\item \textbf{Stability}: Reduce controversial or unintuitive outcomes
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposed System: Dynamic Weighted Voting System (DWVS)}

We propose a straightforward weighted combination of judge and fan percentages:

\begin{equation}
S_{final,i} = \alpha \cdot P_{judge,i} + (1-\alpha) \cdot P_{fan,i}
\label{eq:dwvs}
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $P_{judge,i} = \frac{J_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} J_j}$ is the judge score percentage
\item $P_{fan,i} = \frac{V_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} V_j}$ is the fan vote percentage
\item $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ is the judge weight parameter
\end{itemize}

The contestant with the lowest $S_{final}$ is eliminated.

We conduct comprehensive parameter analysis to identify optimal $\alpha$ values:

\end{figure}

\subsection{Recommendation}

\section{Model Evaluation and Discussion}

\subsection{Strengths of Our Approach}

\textbf{Mathematically Rigorous Inverse Problem Formulation.} By treating fan vote estimation as a constrained optimization problem, we obtain solutions that are guaranteed consistent with observed elimination outcomes. The maximum entropy principle provides a principled way to select among multiple feasible solutions.

\textbf{Comprehensive Multi-Method Analysis.} We apply multiple statistical techniques (correlation, regression, ANOVA, Random Forest) to cross-validate our findings about factor impacts. This triangulation strengthens the reliability of our conclusions.

\textbf{Practical Actionability.} Our proposed DWVS is simple enough for immediate implementation and provides an explicit parameter for production teams to tune based on their priorities.

\subsection{Limitations and Potential Biases}

\textbf{Inherent Solution Non-Uniqueness.} The fan vote estimation problem admits multiple valid solutions. Our estimates represent the ``most uniform'' distribution consistent with eliminations, but actual votes may have been distributed differently.

\textbf{Low Explanatory Power for Fan Votes.} Our regression models explain only 5\% of fan vote variance, indicating that the most important predictors (charisma, pre-existing fanbase) are not captured in the available data.

\textbf{Assumption Dependence.} The judge tiebreaker analysis assumes judges always prefer lower-scoring contestants, which may not reflect actual decision criteria.

\subsection{Directions for Future Work}

\textbf{Social Media Integration.} Incorporating Twitter/Instagram engagement metrics could substantially improve fan vote prediction.

\textbf{Dynamic Modeling.} Tracking contestant popularity trajectories across weeks could capture momentum effects not visible in static analysis.

\textbf{Experimental Validation.} If production data becomes available, our estimated votes could be directly validated against actual figures.
